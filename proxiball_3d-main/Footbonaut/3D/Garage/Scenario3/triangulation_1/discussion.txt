## What you did (and what your results actually mean)

From your report: you built **2D–3D correspondences** from detected AprilTag corners and measured 3D corner coordinates, then solved **PnP per camera** with RANSAC + refinement, and validated with **reprojection error** and a **triangulation round-trip test**. 

That establishes two different notions of “correct”:

* **Image-consistency correct**: ([R|t]) projects your measured 3D tag points onto the observed pixels with very small error.
* **Physical-room correct**: camera centers (C) computed from ([R|t]) match tape-measured room coordinates.

You have strong evidence for the first, but a mismatch on the second.

---

## Why “extrinsics can be correct” while “camera positions are wrong”

### 1) Your extrinsics are correct **in the coordinate frame that your solver thinks is “world”**

`solvePnP` returns (R,t) that map your object/world points to the camera frame:

[
P_c = R,P_w + t
]

Camera center in that same (P_w) frame is:

[
C = -R^T t
]

That math is fine. If (C) looks impossible, the usual reason is:
**the (P_w) you compare against (your “expected positions”) is not the same frame as the (P_w) implied by your 3D tag file and how it was measured.**

Concretely: if your “expected camera position” was measured in *RoomFrame*, but your tag coordinates are effectively in *TagSurveyFrame* that is rotated, mirrored, or translated relative to RoomFrame, then:

* Projections will be excellent (because PnP only cares about consistency between tag 3D and pixels).
* Triangulation will be excellent (because all cameras share the same TagSurveyFrame).
* But computed camera positions will not match your tape-measured RoomFrame.

This is the most common explanation when reprojection is subpixel but camera centers look “outside the room.”

---

### 2) A rigid (or mirrored) transform can preserve all of your “correctness” tests

If your entire 3D tag set is related to the real room by some transform:

* Rigid transform: (P_{room} = R_0 P_{tag} + t_0)
* Or mirrored: includes a reflection (determinant (-1))

Then PnP will produce camera poses in the **tag frame**, not the **room frame**. Triangulation and reprojection still work because everything is self-consistent.

**Your own evidence points to a reflection / axis convention mismatch**:

* camEast has (X,Y) close to expected but (Z) with opposite sign. 
  That pattern is classic when your “up” axis or handedness differs between the two frames you’re comparing.

---

### 3) Planar degeneracy and “pose flips” can give physically implausible camera centers with tiny reprojection

You tested subsets:

* **Floor tags only** → consistent (X,Y), flipped (Z)
* **Wall tags only** → completely wrong pose 

This is a big signal:

* Points on a single plane make PnP more ambiguous (there are known “flip” solutions where the camera ends up on the wrong side of the plane but still reprojects well).
* If your per-camera tag set is dominated by one plane (one wall), the solver can land in a “valid reprojection” solution that is physically wrong unless you strongly constrain with non-coplanar points and cheirality checks.

You do have multiple planes overall (floor + walls), but if the *inliers* RANSAC keeps are mostly from a plane, the effective constraint can still behave planar.

---

## What might actually be wrong in your setup (high-probability list)

### A) Your “expected camera positions” are in a different origin/axis frame than your tag coordinates

Typical causes:

* Origin corner mismatch (NE vs NW vs some offset)
* Axis directions swapped (X↔Y) or inverted (east→west vs west→east)
* You measured camera positions relative to a different reference (wall surface vs corner line vs lens center)
* Units mismatch (cm in one place, mm/m in another)

**Why this fits your symptoms:** reprojection + triangulation are excellent, but physical positions are not.

---

### B) Hidden reflection / handedness mismatch between your room convention and the “world” you assume

Your world definition is right-handed as stated (X, Y, Z). OpenCV camera frame is also right-handed (x right, y down, z forward). But if your measured world coordinates effectively encode a reflected frame (common when surveying along walls), then camera centers will look “behind” planes and Z signs can flip.

**The camEast “Z sign opposite” observation is consistent with this.** 

---

### C) Some 3D tag coordinates (especially wall tags) are wrong in a systematic way

Your subset test showed wall-only calibration is “entirely incorrect.” 
That often happens when:

* Wall plane coordinate is wrong (e.g., Y=298 assumed flat, but wall has offsets like door recess; you mentioned this in earlier context)
* The tag plane normal or corner positions aren’t consistent (tag not perfectly planar to the assumed wall plane; measured corners not matching actual physical placement)
* The “corner coordinates” were measured with small but systematic errors (rotations, tilt, non-orthogonality)

**Important:** small systematic 3D errors can still yield tiny reprojection error if PnP “absorbs” it into pose—especially when you have many points and RANSAC cherry-picks a subset.

---

### D) You are over-trusting subpixel reprojection given very low inlier ratios

You reported inliers/total like 400/2968 (≈14%) for camNorth. 
That means the final pose is supported by a relatively small subset of all collected corners.

If those inliers are:

* concentrated on one plane, or
* concentrated on one region of the image,

you can get:

* extremely low reprojection error on inliers
* but a pose that is not globally physically correct

This does *not* contradict triangulation passing—triangulation uses those same extrinsics.

---

## How to reconcile: “So are the extrinsics correct or not?”

They are **correct for your operational goal** (multi-camera 3D ball reconstruction) *in the tag-defined coordinate system*.

They are **not yet validated as correct in the physical room coordinate system** you used for “expected positions.”

That’s why both can be true at once.

## Concrete improvements / fixes (in order of impact)

### 1) Solve the frame alignment explicitly (recommended)

Treat your PnP world as **TagFrame**, and your tape-measured as **RoomFrame**.

Compute a rigid (or similarity) transform (T) such that:

[
P_{room} \approx T , P_{tag}
]

How to get (T):

* Use several known reference points measured in both frames (e.g., 3–6 tag centers whose room coordinates you trust).
* Fit (R_0,t_0) (and optionally scale) via least squares.
* Then transform camera centers: (C_{room} = R_0 C_{tag} + t_0)

If the best fit requires a reflection (determinant (-1)), you’ve found the handedness issue.

---

### 2) Add cheirality validation and reject “behind-camera” solutions

After PnP, check that most points satisfy:

[
Z_c > 0
]

If large portions are behind camera, you likely have a flipped solution.

---

### 3) Improve 3D geometry conditioning per camera

For each camera, ensure inliers span **multiple planes / depth variations**:

* At least one floor tag + several wall tags visible simultaneously
* Avoid calibrating a camera from mostly one wall plane

---

### 4) Joint multi-camera bundle adjustment (best long-term)

Instead of independent per-camera PnP, solve for all camera poses jointly minimizing total reprojection across all cameras, optionally with priors (e.g., cameras near walls, Z≈known height). This reduces “weird but reprojection-good” local minima.

---

### 5) Fix the measurement model for walls with recesses (door offsets)

If some tags lie on recessed/offset regions, encode that in 3D coordinates (don’t force all to the same Y plane). Otherwise you inject systematic errors that will distort pose.

---

## Direct answer to your main question

### “How come extrinsics are correct if camera positions are completely wrong?”

Because your validation (reprojection + triangulation) proves that the extrinsics are **self-consistent** with the **3D tag coordinate system you provided**, not that they match the **physical room coordinate system you expected**. A frame mismatch (rotation/translation/reflection), planar pose ambiguity, or systematic 3D survey errors can make camera centers look impossible while preserving near-perfect reprojection and good triangulation in that same internal frame.
